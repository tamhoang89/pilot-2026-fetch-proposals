### Who else solves this today - competitors/alternatives, and why does your approach win?

No basket or index protocol exists on Cardano today, so our competition is what users settle for instead.

- Manual portfolios. The default: buy eight assets on a DEX, track them in a spreadsheet, rebalance by hand. Free, but slow, fee-heavy and rarely maintained.
- Indigo Protocol. Synthetic exposure through collateralised debt, so the user carries debt and liquidation risk. A Darwin basket is spot, fully backed and redeemable into the underlying.
- EVM index protocols such as Index Coop. Mature, but on other chains, and none can hold CIP-113 programmable tokens.

Our edge is not features. Compliant baskets of programmable tokens are only possible where compliance lives in the ledger.

### Please provide details about the Technology Readiness Level selected for your existing product

Darwin is live at [app.darwin.market](http://app.darwin.market), running on a public testnet. It is a complete, integrated system: a user connects a wallet, is provisioned an account, funds it, mints a basket token, holds diversified exposure as one token, tracks NAV, and redeems into the underlying.

The implementation has four layers. A Next.js frontend handles wallet connection, basket selection and the mint and redeem flow. Onchain, vault contracts custody the constituents, a NAV module derives value from oracle feeds, and a rebalancing engine detects drift.

### What is your on-chain architecture, and why is it the right fit for selected integration(s) and this area of interest's technical requirements?

Darwin runs live today in four layers: a Next.js frontend, an offchain service building transactions and indexing state, onchain vault contracts with a NAV module and rebalancing engine, and adapters isolating oracle and bridge dependencies. The Cardano work replaces the onchain layer and adapters; frontend and offchain service are reused.

On Cardano the onchain layer is six Aiken validators, built around one principle: never put shared mutable state in a single UTxO. basket_registry holds per-basket config as a reference input. basket_vault custodies constituents, sharded across UTxOs. order_validator holds mint and redeem intents, settled in bulk by a batcher. basket_token_policy governs supply. rebalance_manager runs drift detection and the swap order lifecycle. nav_module derives NAV.

A single global vault would serialise every user into one transaction per block, so we shard state and batch intents. In exchange, eUTXO gives deterministic fees and ledger-level multi-asset accounting.

Oracles are not a single-provider dependency. The NAV module reads any feed exposing an inline datum as a reference input, so Charli3 and Orcfax are interchangeable behind one adapter, with a staleness bound halting mints rather than pricing stale data. Stablecoins: baskets are denominated in USDM and USDCx. Programmable tokens: the index token is issued under CIP-113, so eligibility is ledger-enforced on every transfer.

### What solution are you building, and what specific problem does it solve - for whom?

Cardano's binding constraint is liquidity. An index protocol is one of the few product types that concentrates it rather than consuming it: every mint locks the underlying constituents into a vault, so capital accumulates instead of being routed away. That is the reason we are proposing this now.

Darwin is an onchain basket and RWA protocol. One token holds a diversified portfolio, NAV is computed onchain, and the protocol rebalances toward target weights. Mint in one action, redeem into the underlying at any time.

Getting diversified exposure onchain is otherwise a manual chore: research a dozen assets, execute a dozen transactions, pay a dozen fees, then rebalance by hand as weights drift. Traditional finance solved this with the fund, now over $15 trillion globally. Cardano has no equivalent primitive.

Who has this problem: Cardano DeFi users who cannot buy a diversified position in one transaction; treasuries that need exposure without operational overhead; and RWA issuers building on CIP-113 who will hold compliant assets with no product to distribute them through.

We ship two classes. Permissionless baskets hold ADA, native blue chips and stablecoins such as USDM and USDCx. CIP-113 gated baskets hold tokenized equities and RWAs, with investor eligibility enforced by the ledger on every transfer rather than by our frontend, so a regulated basket stays compliant even when the token moves outside our app.

Live today at [app.darwin.market](http://app.darwin.market).

### Please provide details about the Technology Readiness Level selected for the integration you're proposing

The Cardano integration is a formulated technology concept, specified against the target primitives rather than assumed.

The specification maps each Darwin component onto eUTXO: an order-based mint and redeem design that avoids UTxO contention, NAV derived from Charli3 feeds consumed as reference inputs through their Aiken library, and rebalancing modelled on the Minswap V2 batcher lifecycle. For CIP-113 it accounts for the implementation as built, including ownership by stake credential at a shared script address, transfer validation through the global validator and registered substandards, and the absence of DEX support for programmable tokens.

Implementation, deployment and testing in a Cardano environment are the work this grant funds.
