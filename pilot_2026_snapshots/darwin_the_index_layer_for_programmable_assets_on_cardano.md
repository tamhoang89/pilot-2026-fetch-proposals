### Who else solves this today - competitors/alternatives, and why does your approach win?

No basket or index protocol exists on Cardano today, so our competition is what users settle for instead.

- Manual portfolios. The default: buy eight assets on a DEX, track them in a spreadsheet, rebalance by hand. Free, but slow, fee-heavy and rarely maintained.
- Indigo Protocol. Synthetic exposure through collateralised debt, so the user carries debt and liquidation risk. A Darwin basket is spot, fully backed and redeemable into the underlying.
- EVM index protocols such as Index Coop. Mature, but on other chains, and none can hold CIP-113 programmable tokens.

Our edge is not features. Compliant baskets of programmable tokens are only possible where compliance lives in the ledger.

### What is your on-chain architecture, and why is it the right fit for selected integration(s) and this area of interest's technical requirements?

Darwin runs live today in four layers: a Next.js frontend, an offchain service that builds transactions and indexes state, onchain vault contracts with a NAV module and rebalancing engine, and adapters isolating oracle and bridge dependencies. The Cardano work replaces the onchain layer and the adapters; frontend and offchain service are reused.

On Cardano the onchain layer is six Aiken validators, built around one principle: never put shared mutable state in a single UTxO. basket_registry holds per-basket config as a reference input. basket_vault custodies constituents, sharded across UTxOs. order_validator holds user mint and redeem intents, settled in bulk by a batcher. basket_token_policy governs supply. rebalance_manager runs drift detection and the swap order lifecycle. nav_module derives NAV.

A single global vault would serialise every user into one transaction per block, so we shard state and batch intents. In exchange, eUTXO gives deterministic fees and ledger-level multi-asset accounting, so an index is a native multi-asset object rather than a contract emulating one.

Integration fit. Oracles: NAV and drift read Charli3 feeds as reference inputs via their Aiken library, with a staleness bound halting mints rather than pricing stale data. Stablecoins: baskets are denominated in USDM and USDCx. Programmable tokens: the index token is issued under CIP-113, so eligibility is ledger-enforced on every transfer, and the vault integrates it as a compliant holder.

### What solution are you building, and what specific problem does it solve - for whom?

Getting diversified exposure onchain is still a manual chore. To hold a theme, you research a dozen assets, execute a dozen transactions, pay a dozen fees, then rebalance by hand as weights drift. Traditional finance solved this with the ETF, now over $15 trillion globally. Cardano has no equivalent. There is no index or basket product on the network.

Darwin is an onchain basket protocol. One token holds a diversified portfolio, NAV is computed onchain from Charli3 feeds, and the protocol rebalances toward target weights. Mint in one action, redeem into the underlying assets at any time.

We ship two classes. Permissionless baskets hold ADA, native blue chips and stablecoins such as USDM and USDCx, and go live on mainnet. CIP-113 gated baskets hold tokenized equities and RWAs, with investor eligibility enforced by the ledger on every transfer rather than by our frontend, so a regulated basket stays compliant even when the token moves outside our app.

Who has this problem: holders who want one-click thematic exposure instead of assembling a portfolio; treasuries and funds that need diversified positions without operational overhead; and RWA issuers on Cardano who have compliant assets but no product to distribute them through.

Cardano handles multi-asset accounting at the ledger layer, so a basket here is a genuinely native multi-asset object rather than a wrapper contract emulating one.

### Please provide details about the Technology Readiness Level selected for the integration you're proposing

The Cardano integration is a formulated technology concept, specified against the target primitives rather than assumed.

The specification maps each Darwin component onto eUTXO: an order-based mint and redeem design that avoids UTxO contention, NAV derived from Charli3 feeds consumed as reference inputs through their Aiken library, and rebalancing modelled on the Minswap V2 batcher lifecycle. For CIP-113 it accounts for the implementation as built, including ownership by stake credential at a shared script address, transfer validation through the global validator and registered substandards, and the absence of DEX support for programmable tokens.

Implementation, deployment and testing in a Cardano environment are the work this grant funds.
