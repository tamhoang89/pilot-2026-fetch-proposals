Today, users mainly rely on traditional certificates, PDFs, university records, training-provider databases and professional platforms such as LinkedIn to demonstrate their skills. Rather than replacing existing certificates immediately, GenderChain adds a portable and independently verifiable digital layer.

### What is your on-chain architecture, and why is it the right fit for selected integration(s) and this area of interest's technical requirements?

GenderChain Identity will use a Cardano wallet-based architecture centred on CIP-0170 on-chain identity. 

The wallet acts as the user's cryptographic identity anchor, while the platform maintains the user interface, skills profile and credential workflow off-chain. 

Only the minimum verifiable information required for proof and verification will be referenced on-chain, avoiding unnecessary personal data exposure.

The architecture will include: (1) Cardano wallet connection for identity and authentication; (2) an off-chain application layer for profiles, skills and user management; (3) a credential issuance workflow linked to verified learning activities; (4) Cardano transactions providing tamper-evident evidence of credential events; and (5) a public verification layer allowing authorised third parties to validate credentials.

This architecture is appropriate because CIP-0170 directly supports the project's primary integration: verifiable on-chain identity. It separates sensitive data from blockchain records while retaining independently verifiable evidence. 

The system will be validated on testnet before mainnet deployment and instrumented to measure genuine user activity, transaction evidence and adoption.

### What is your business model, and what keeps this running after the pilot? Who pays, and why does usage continue once grant funding ends?

GenderChain Identity will use a B2B/B2B2C model. Basic access will remain free for women and individual learners, while universities, training organisations, NGOs, employers and innovation hubs will pay for institutional credential issuance, cohort management, verification services and customised programmes.

The pilot grant will finance product development, initial onboarding and validation, not permanent operations. After the pilot, recurring revenue will come from institutional subscriptions, sponsored learning cohorts, credential services and ecosystem partnerships.

Organisations will continue using the platform because it provides portable, verifiable proof of skills, reduces certificate verification costs and improves the credibility of their training programmes. This creates a sustainable model where institutions finance continued operations while priority beneficiaries retain low-barrier access.

### What solution are you building, and what specific problem does it solve - for whom?

GenderChain Identity is a Cardano-based digital identity and skills platform for women and young people in Eastern DRC. It addresses a key problem: digital skills gained through training are often difficult to prove, verify and carry across organisations because certificates remain mainly paper or PDF-based.

The platform will allow users to connect a Cardano wallet, create a verifiable skills profile and receive digital credentials linked to verified learning achievements. Third parties such as employers, universities, NGOs and training organisations will be able to verify these credentials.

The solution combines local-language onboarding, gender inclusion and blockchain-based verification to turn learning achievements into portable, verifiable digital evidence, helping women and young people gain greater visibility and access to digital opportunities.
