### What is your on-chain architecture, and why is it the right fit for selected integration(s) and this area of interest's technical requirements?

Kaltic already uses a hybrid architecture in which detailed agricultural records remain off-chain while cryptographic evidence is anchored to Cardano. The current MVP already creates structured traceability records, canonicalizes data, generates SHA-256 digests, submits Cardano transactions and links them back to Digital Product Passports. This provides the technical foundation for CIP-0170.

Each participating organization will be associated with a KERI AID and Key Event Log (KEL). Authority will be established through the CIP-0170 AUTH_BEGIN flow and credential chain.

For each traceability event, Kaltic will reuse its existing processing pipeline to create application metadata and a deterministic digest. The digest will be anchored in the organization’s KEL and referenced by a CIP-0170 ATTEST transaction under metadata label 170, while associated traceability metadata remains separately linked through its digest.

Transactions will be submitted from each organization’s external Cardano wallet. Kaltic will index the transaction, retrieve the relevant KEL, validate the credential chain and attestation, and display the claim as verified in the Digital Product Passport.

This fits Kaltic because CIP-0170 extends the existing integrity layer with verifiable organizational accountability without redesigning the core product or exposing sensitive operational data on-chain.

### Who is your target market, and what evidence shows real demand/product-market fit?

Kaltic’s initial target market is Mexican agricultural producers, farms, packers, and other supply-chain operators serving regulated and export-oriented markets, especially the United States.

The market is significant: U.S. agricultural imports from Mexico averaged about **$43.8B annually in 2021–2025** (USDA ERS: <https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/ag-and-food-statistics-charting-the-essentials/agricultural-trade>). In horticulture, Mexico supplied **63% of U.S. vegetable imports and 47% of fruit and nut imports in 2023** (USDA ERS: <https://www.ers.usda.gov/amber-waves/2024/october/growth-in-mexico-s-horticultural-exports-to-the-united-states-continued-even-as-new-u-s-food-safety-laws-took-effect>).

Demand is reinforced by regulation. FDA’s Food Traceability Rule (FSMA 204) requires additional traceability records for covered foods, including foreign firms supplying the U.S. market (FDA: <https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods>).

Kaltic is at the pilot-validation stage, with a working agricultural traceability MVP already built. The combination of substantial U.S.-Mexico agricultural trade and mandatory traceability requirements creates a clear commercial need for tools that help supply-chain operators capture, exchange and verify traceability records efficiently.

### What solution are you building, and what specific problem does it solve - for whom?

**Kaltic is a digital traceability platform for agricultural producers, packers and exporters operating in regulated supply chains. It helps them create, maintain and share structured traceability records .**

**Today, critical events such as harvesting, receiving, packing and transformation are often recorded across spreadsheets, documents and disconnected systems. This makes it difficult for buyers, auditors and supply-chain partners to independently verify both that a record has not been altered and which organization is accountable for it.**

**Through this project, Kaltic will integrate CIP-0170 on-chain identity attestations, enabling participating organizations to cryptographically attest to the traceability records they are responsible for and link those claims to a persistent, verifiable organizational identity on Cardano.**
